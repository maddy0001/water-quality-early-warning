Wiring the Uno – one part at a time

Touch sensor first, then DHT11, then the water probe. 9 wires in total. The check program is ewsv2\hardware\ewsv2_hwcheck; the early-warning program (Segment 8) is ewsv2\edge\firmware\ewsv2_edge. Both use exactly this wiring.

First: is VERSION 2 on the board?

The first lines in the Serial Monitor after pressing the Uno's reset button must say === ewsv2 hardware check VERSION 2 === and Pins: touch SIG=D3 VCC=D4 | DHT11 DATA=A1 VCC=5V | probe AO=A0 VCC=A2. If an error line mentions D2 or D7, the old program is still on the board and cannot see the DHT11 or the probe on their new pins. Open ewsv2\hardware\ewsv2_hwcheck\ewsv2_hwcheck.ino from the repository and upload it again.

Your step A result: correct

Your output shows the board, the upload and the Serial Monitor all work. With nothing wired, PROBE ... NOT DETECTED and DHT11 NO ANSWER are the expected answers. TOUCH TOUCHED appears because pin D3 has nothing on it yet: an empty pin reads random values. That goes away once the touch sensor is connected.

0Before you start

  1. Unplug the USB cable from the Uno. Every time you add or move a wire.
  2. Keep the wires that are soldered to your touch sensor and DHT11. This page uses exactly those colours.
  3. Keep the 2 wires between the fork and the small blue probe board (white and black). They are fine.
  4. A wire's colour means nothing electrically. On your DHT11 the red wire is GND and the brown one is VCC; on the touch sensor black is VCC. That is unusual, so follow the tables, not habit.

1Make 9 single wires and 9 single pins

  1. Wires: the touch sensor and the DHT11 already have their wires. Only the probe board needs 3 extra wires: take purple, grey and blue from your extra wires (any 3 colours work; write down which is which).
  2. Pins: from the long black strip of pins, snap off 9 single pins (bend between two pins until it breaks).
  3. Why the pins: look at the free end of each wire. If it ends in a small black plastic hole, it cannot go into the Uno's holes directly. Push a single pin into the Uno hole (longer metal side down), then slide the wire's hole onto the pin sticking up. If the wire feels loose, put the pin in the other way round. If a wire ends in a metal pin, push it straight into the Uno hole; no extra pin needed.
  4. The sensor boards already have pins sticking out, so wires slide straight onto them.

2Find the 9 holes on the Uno

Turn the Uno so the USB socket is on the left. Read the small white labels printed next to each row of holes.

EdgeHoles used (by their printed label)
Top edgeGND (just left of 13), 4, 3
Bottom edge, left5V, GND, GND (the two GND holes next to each other)
Bottom edge, rightA0, A1, A2

Your clone may print labels in a slightly different place, but the names are the same. Always go by the printed name. If you can't read a label, use your phone camera zoom.

3Touch sensor – 3 wires to the top edge

Your touch sensor already has 3 wires: yellow = SIG, black = VCC, white = GND.

Test: plug in the USB. The Serial Monitor shows TOUCH now not touched. Touch the round pad: the small LED on the touch board lights, and touches counted goes up by 1 each time.

4DHT11 – 3 wires to the bottom edge

Unplug the USB first. Your DHT11 already has 3 wires: brown = VCC, white = DATA, red = GND. Careful: here red is GND, not power.

WireDHT11 pinUno hole
whiteDATAA1, bottom edge
brownVCC5V, bottom edge
redGND (yes, red is GND here)GND, bottom edge (the first GND, right next to 5V)

Test: plug in the USB. The DHT11's red LED lights, and within 4 seconds the Serial Monitor shows DHT11 28 C, 65 % humidity -> OK (your numbers). Breathe on it from close by for 3 seconds: the humidity goes up, then slowly back down.

If the DHT11 board gets warm or the Uno's ON LED goes out: unplug at once. VCC and GND are swapped: brown must be on 5V and red on GND.

5Water probe – 3 wires to the bottom edge

Unplug the USB first. The small blue probe board has a screw knob, 2 pins on one side (already going to the fork) and 4 pins on the other side, printed VCC, GND, DO, AO. We use 3 of the 4; DO stays empty.

WireProbe board pinUno hole
purpleVCCA2, bottom edge
greyGNDGND, bottom edge (the second GND)
blueAOA0, bottom edge
–DOnothing

VCC goes to A2, not to 5V, on purpose: the program switches the probe on only for a moment every 2 seconds, so the fork corrodes much more slowly in water. The board's power LED blinks briefly every 2 seconds.

Test (USB plugged in):

  1. Fork in the air: PROBE value 1000+ (off: 0) -> connected, DRY (in air).
  2. Dip only the two prongs about 2 cm into a glass of tap water. Hold the fork by its black top; keep the board and wires dry. The value drops (WET). Write the number down.
  3. Add a spoon of salt, stir and wait 10 s. The value drops further. Write it down. This is our "water event".
  4. Pull one fork wire off while the fork is in the water. The value jumps back to dry. This is our "sensor broke" case.
  5. Rinse and dry the fork.
